# Small teams buy looks, build feel

The viral near-AAA Unreal clips are not "AI-made games": **coding agents write the logic through MCP servers, while bought or scanned assets, UE5's renderer and hand placement supply the look**. The animation stack is mostly free from Epic (GASP motion matching, Lyra) plus Mixamo, warping layers and one paid melee pack. Ready-made combat frameworks are built on GAS or Blueprints, which clashes with our rules, so we should mine them for patterns rather than adopt them. The strongest gun-feel evidence, Naughty Dog's GDC 2024 talk, says feel is "almost entirely hit reactions" on accurate hit collision; for us, hit capsules and damage states beat camera shake. Enemy variety stays cheap with one C++ base, a data asset per archetype and shared damage states. Re-implementing another game's mechanics is generally lawful under US copyright; copying its art, animations, sounds, distinctive characters or layouts is not, and ripped assets are the most common cause of takedowns. Our first jobs are to read the Lyra and GASP licence terms, since Lyra clips are already in use, and to fix the 5.8 MetaHuman retargeting bugs that hit our `MHC_` enemies. This report is US-centric and not legal advice; trademark and patent law were not researched.

## Viral clips are agent-written logic on bought, scanned and hand-placed art

Many of the 2026 clips credit Epic's own MCP server. "Unreal MCP" (plugin identifier `ModelContextProtocol`) **first shipped with UE 5.8 as Experimental**. It lets Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Gemini CLI drive the editor through toolsets. The server runs at `http://127.0.0.1:8000/mcp`. By default it exposes only four meta-tools (`list_toolsets`, `describe_toolsets`, `describe_tools`, `call_tool`). Tool calls run **one at a time on the game thread**, and there is no authentication beyond a localhost bind ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-mcp-in-unreal-editor)). A vendor of a competing MCP server says the toolset is deliberately minimal, makes each tool call its own undo entry, and offers limited read-back ([StraySpark blog](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/epic-official-mcp-plugin-ue5-8-vs-third-party)). That matches our rule to read back or screenshot every change. Secondary sources disagree on the ship date, giving June 17 ([3daistudio](https://www.3daistudio.com/3d-generator-ai-comparison-alternatives-guide/unreal-mcp)) and June 23, 2026 ([VP Land](https://www.vp-land.com/stories/unreal-engine-5-8-embeds-an-mcp-server-so-ai-agents-can-drive-the-editor)).

The most candid creator disclosure shows the real division of labour. "I Built My Dream Game in 72 Hours — Assets by AI, Gameplay by Claude Code" is a UE 5.8 platformer. **Tripo and Hunyuan generated the 3D assets, Blender handled rigging and assembly, and Claude Code wrote the logic**: collectibles, lever doors, health and damage zones. **Lights, lanterns and props were placed by hand**, because neither Claude Code nor Codex could place things reliably from screenshots. Precise prompts ("slide along the X axis") beat vague ones ([daily.dev summary](https://daily.dev/posts/i-built-my-dream-game-in-72-hours-assets-by-ai-gameplay-by-claude-code-eedcr1c11) of the [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k9cbm5jSOxk)). A second write-up, of an endless runner built through the community "Unreal Claude" and VibeUE plugins, found the logic fine but the Blueprints messy ([daily.dev](https://daily.dev/posts/claude-code-took-over-unreal-engine-5-and-built-a-game-lsf8camb8)).

The bigger viral posts are self-reported, seen here only through search summaries, and unverified. Stefan 3D AI's post (about 995.5K views) claims a one-week open world in UE 5.9 with five races and 16 combat quests. Buried in it are **$6,528 of model tokens, five top-tier subscriptions plus another on a 24 GB-VRAM cloud PC**, 119 Tripo models and 1000+ images. No breakdown and no playable build were found ([X post](https://x.com/Stefan_3D_AI/status/2125143321098264592)). Pat Simmons's "build games just by talking to Claude" demo leans on a city cloned from Google Earth with Cesium and on custom Blender buildings ([X post](https://x.com/per_simmons_/status/2069588907801424263)). In both, non-AI data does much of the visual work. The pre-AI benchmark for "looks too real", the bodycam shooter Unrecord, was **handcrafted from Megascans, marketplace assets and bespoke assets** ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/pc-gaming/unrecord-the-game-that-looked-too-real-to-be-true-until-now)). IGN downloaded the same compiled UE5 level assets used in the trailer ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/unrecord-dev-posts-noclip-video-to-prove-the-realistic-bodycam-fps-isnt-fake)).

The pattern behind the clips follows from this. "Built a game" means a vertical slice. Store, scanned and real-world data gets credited as "AI". Token spend and manual placement are buried. The photoreal look comes from assets plus Lumen, Nanite and VSM, and no source quantifies how much each contributes. Our Claude Code + Unreal MCP + VibeUE setup is the documented pattern. Agents earn their keep on C++, StateTree tasks and batch editor operations. Set dressing, lighting and composition remain human work checked against reference screenshots. AI video mocap is not yet a reliable shortcut either: Rokoko itself notes inconsistent accuracy and occlusion problems with monocular video ([Rokoko](https://www.rokoko.com/insights/the-future-of-motion-capture)).

## Free Epic animation plus three warping layers carry most of the polish

### GASP is the locomotion base, and the cost of motion matching is smaller than it looks

GASP is free on Fab. It ships **500+ game-ready mocap animations** on the UEFN Mannequin, and Epic's blog says you may use them "with or without their accompanying animation Blueprints and retarget them to your own characters" ([Epic blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/blog/game-animation-sample)). That wording is a blog statement, not licence text. The motion matching works like this: Pose Search databases are queried from the character's movement model, a Pose History node supplies the trajectory, and **Chooser tables limit which databases are eligible** by gameplay context. The docs say that filtering also saves CPU. Traversal (vault, climb, drop) runs as montages, and a sandbox switches between MetaHuman body types ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-animation-sample-project-in-unreal-engine)). The 5.7 update added the experimental Mover plugin and a slide ([Epic tech blog 5.7](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/tech-blog/explore-the-updates-to-the-game-animation-sample-project-in-ue-5-7)). A 5.8 build is dated 12 Aug 2026 ([Epic tech blog 5.8](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/download-the-latest-game-animation-sample-project-now-updated-for-ue-5-8)). Nothing in the sources shows GASP covering sword combat or more than basic aiming, so we should plan for melee and shooter layers from elsewhere. MIT-licensed community variants exist: GASP Refactored, a C++ rework with ALS overlays that lists UE 5.6 support ([GitHub](https://github.com/Spr-Aachen/Unreal-MotionMatchingSystem)), and GASP-ALS, which adds ALS overlay layering ([GitHub](https://github.com/gokhocse/GASP-ALS)). The C++ rework fits our "logic in C++" rule better than the original, but its 5.8 compatibility is unchecked.

The only per-character numbers come from one community Unreal Insights profile on UE 5.7, hardware unstated, with both scenes GPU-bound ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/state-machine-vs-motion-matching-demo-performance-analysis-share/2721152)):

| Per character | State machine (19 chars) | GASP motion matching (1 char) |
|---|---|---|
| Locomotion selection | 0.008 ms | ~0.25 ms |
| Worker-thread anim eval | ~0.43 ms | 0.50 ms |
| Game-thread anim | ~0.07 ms | ~0.14 ms |

Pose selection is about 31x more expensive, but **total animation cost per character barely moves**. In the state-machine scene, PoseDriver (0.21 ms per character) and Control Rig (0.08 ms) cost more than locomotion did. Extrapolated to ten enemies, motion matching adds roughly 2.5 ms of aggregate worker-thread time, spread across cores. That figure is an extrapolation, not a measurement, and the author says Update Rate Optimization reduces it. Epic names channel count and sample rate as the main cost levers ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine)), and GASP includes a database LOD toggle for dense versus sparse data. **No data exists for Apple Silicon or the RTX 3070 Ti laptop**, so any choice here must be verified with `stat` and Insights on machines A and B. A defensible starting point, which is our own judgment and unsourced, is motion matching for the player and the cheaper Lyra-style state machine with warping for enemies. Enemies would move to sparse motion-matching databases only if A/B measurements leave room.

### Retargeting to our MetaHumans has two known 5.8-era traps

Epic's documented route goes IK Rig, then Auto Create Retarget Chains (MetaHuman skeletons come pre-set for it), then Auto Create IK, then IK Retargeter ([Epic MetaHuman docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/create-a-custom-ik-retargeter)). GASP retargets at runtime with `Retarget Pose From Mesh` and `RTG_UEFN_to_MetaHuman_nrw` ([Epic MetaHuman docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/runtime-retargeting)). Two problems have been reported. In 5.7, auto-generation is sensitive to meshes from the new MetaHuman Creator, the `SKM_MHC` meshes ([forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/animation-retargeting-broken-on-new-skm-mhc-metahuman-skeletal-mesh-ue-5-7/2720169)). In 5.8, MetaHuman wrists stay bent at runtime ("hook hands") even though the preview looks fine. The **reported fix is Forced LOD Model = 1 on the hidden source mesh `CharacterMesh0`**, which can otherwise sit at a LOD with no finger bones ([forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/gameanimationsample-5-8-bad-retargeting-to-metahuman/2837097)). Our Guard, Interceptor, Flanker, Heavy and Donk are all `MHC_` MetaHumans on 5.8, so both traps likely apply. Runtime retargeting also means a hidden source mesh and a retarget node on every NPC. We therefore recommend offline-retargeting the Lyra and GASP clips to the MetaHuman skeleton for enemies; this is our judgment, not a sourced benchmark. UE 5.6 also changed the retargeter to an op stack. Users reported different results after the change and fixed failed auto-retargets by building the rig by hand ([forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/unable-to-retarget-animation-to-metahuman-unreal-5-6/2539409)). Mixamo clips need the community "Mixamo Animation Retargeting V2" tool, which was updated for that op stack ([forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/mixamo-animation-retargeting-v2-for-unreal-engine-5-and-metahuman/552648?page=3)).

### Warping turns a small clip set into AAA-looking movement and melee

Three Epic systems make a small clip set look like AAA movement and melee. **Pose Warping** (in the Animation Warping plugin) provides Stride Warping, which matches stride to capsule speed; Orientation Warping, which turns the legs toward the movement direction while the spine holds the facing; and Slope Warping ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine)). Epic notes that warping covers more movement with fewer animations ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/locomotion-in-unreal-engine)). **Distance Matching** drives starts and stops by distance to target. **Motion Warping** bends root motion toward a target inside notify windows on a montage ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)). Blueprint can also create a window at runtime with "Add Root Motion Modifier Skew Warp" ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)). Indie souls-likes rely on the last one. A student team adapted Epic's 5.5 motion-matching sample and advises "motion warping and generous hit boxes" ([Hollowborn report](https://gradex.staffs.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/5055_Hollowborn-Dev-Report.pdf)). A solo prototype lists animation warping, blend spaces and AnimBPs for both player and AI ([Coello prototype](https://sites.google.com/view/albertocoello/games/souls-like-prototype)). If the draft canon opening becomes playable, the hero stabbing the guard is exactly this case: a montage warped to align with a target.

### Melee and filler animation come from a handful of licensed sources

For melee and filler clips the licensed options are well known, though their prices and licence tiers were mostly seen only in snippets. **Mixamo is royalty-free for commercial games.** You may not redistribute the raw files, and no credit is required ([Adobe FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/en/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html); [Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/mixamo-discussions/mixamo-faq-licensing-royalties-ownership-eula-and-tos/td-p/13234775)). Kubold's FAQ says its licence is **personal and non-transferable** ([Kubold](https://www.kubold.com/unreal-faq-2)), which on a 4-person team may mean one seat per user. The only verified price is a Unity listing: $50 for 205 longsword mocap clips ([Unity Asset Store](https://marketplace.unity.com/packages/3d/animations/longsword-animset-pro-92239)). Other sources include Fab sword packs such as a 417-clip one-handed set ([Fab](https://fab.com/s/e2d9de6366c4)), Game Dev Hero's 2025 UE5 sword packs ([itch.io](https://itch.io/devlog/966242/added-new-animation-pack-sword-and-shield-animations-v1.amp)), and the legacy Frank packs, at least one of which is vault-only ([Marketplace](https://www.unrealengine.com/marketplace/ja/product/frank-action-rpg-sword-1-basic-set)). Cascadeur covers custom hits, reactions and finishers, and its blog spotlights solo UE5 melee projects made with it ([Cascadeur](https://cascadeur.com/blog/view/indie-project-spotlight-og-realistic-combat-system)). No source gives hours or weeks for a small team to reach AAA-feeling locomotion and combat, so this report estimates none.

## Frameworks are reference code; hit reactions are the real gun feel

### Every serious kit conflicts with our C++/no-GAS/StateTree rules

| Kit | What it is | Fit for us |
|---|---|---|
| ACF V4 ($174.99) | Multi-module C++, melee + ranged, GAS-based, lock-on, hit-stop, AI ticketing, GASP integration | Requires a GAS decision; RPG systems are dead weight |
| ACF Ultimate 4.6 ($349.99 seen; a snippet says from $699.99) | Adds melee/ranged AI groups, guns, procedural first-person weapon animation (11/2026) | Same GAS conflict; useful as reference code |
| DCS Bundle ($32.99) | Blueprint components: lock-on, dodge, parry, backstab, guns | Last dated update July 2023 (UE 5.2) |
| Soulslike Framework | 250+ Blueprints, "simplified GAS", no motion matching, Mac untested | Blueprint-only, against our rules |
| Epic 5.6 template variants (free) | Third-Person "Combat" (combos, targeting, AI) and First-Person "Arena Shooter" (pickups, bullet types, AI, ammo UI) | Lowest-risk reference; not confirmed to still ship in 5.8 |

Sources: [ACF V4](https://www.fab.com/listings/ca5f015f-4976-4928-84a1-8826954a2a57), [ACF Ultimate](https://fab.com/listings/cc258205-8fcf-41e5-9b48-6ec44e46d7eb), [Fab category snippet](https://www.fab.com/category/tool-and-plugin/game-mechanics?lang=en), [DCS](https://www.fab.com/listings/82994e04-68bb-4434-bf95-763d340a5029), [SLF thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/soulslike-framework-complete-soulslike-kit/2443346), [Epic templates](https://www.unrealengine.com/news/updated-game-templates-for-unreal-engine-5-6available-now).

**None of these listings states UE 5.8 support**, and none renders its licence tiers. **No page read documents how they implement i-frames, stamina or parry windows**, and ACF's page never mentions stamina, dodge or parry at all. What the sources do give are the reusable patterns. Use montage sections with notifies that run capsule traces for melee hits ([Epic answers](https://answers.unrealengine.com/questions/43365/fortnite-melee-combat-system.html)). Freeze the montage on the attacker and the victim for hit-stop instead of using global time dilation ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-can-i-create-hitstop-system-base-on-different-section-of-the-montage/298007); [Hollowborn report](https://gradex.staffs.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/5055_Hollowborn-Dev-Report.pdf)). Buffer the dodge input until the attack's cancel window opens ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/root-lock-stays-on-momentarily-when-changing-animation-with-a-notify/372932)). The warnings are anecdotal. One Reddit commenter (seen only via a snippet) argues that once you customise a kit deeply you might as well have built your own ([Reddit mirror](https://redlib.hackliberty.org/r/unrealengine/comments/1iurzgs/soulslike_framework_complete_soulslike_kit)). A marketplace seller adds that a game's specific feel "won't be accomplished by gluing together marketplace assets" ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/about-combat-framework-systems-in-marketplace/1414831)). Lyra advice runs the same way: build the game as a GameFeature plugin, don't edit Lyra, and treat "only the Lyra C++" as reusable and its binary assets as examples ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/best-practices-to-build-upon-lyra/515743/29)). Most of Lyra's weapon logic lives in `B_WeaponInstance_Base` ([x157](https://x157.github.io/UE5/LyraStarterGame/Weapons)).

### Naughty Dog's GDC talk ranks hit reactions above every other feel layer

The one strong primary source on gun feel is Derek Mattson's GDC 2024 talk on The Last of Us Part II. He lists VFX, sound, fire rate, camera shake, kick and haptics, then says weapon feel "is **almost entirely hit reactions**". A leg shot gets a matching stagger, and a neck shot gets a unique death even though gameplay counts it as a headshot. That depends on accurate collision. Naughty Dog **replaced rigid torso boxes with horizontal capsules at the spine joints** and split the thigh in two so knee shots reliably trigger the leg stun. Mattson's advice is to start "with a good base of animation and hit detection", because otherwise FX and sound just add noise. The **leg-shot stun** (instant, low damage) opened up tactical options, such as a grab, a meat shield or a follow-up strike, without adding an enemy type. For third-person aim he used three traces. A camera probe, an environment-ignoring spherecast, finds the target. A "secret" raycast prevents accidental wall hits. The character's projectile is back-calculated so it collides only with enemies. To forgive input latency (at 30 fps with a multi-frame pipeline, what the player saw can be up to 3 frames, 0.06–0.1 s, old), he stores the reticle-overlap percentage for the last few frames, rolls against that stored value on a miss, and remaps the result so "95% == 100%". Pistols are hitscan, and "everything else that lingers is just feedback" ([GDC notes](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Mattson_Derek_ASecondShot_Notes.pdf)).

Lower-authority sources fill in the ordering. Recoil kick exists partly so that re-centring the crosshair becomes a skill ([The Astronauts](https://www.theastronauts.com/2019/03/making-gun-come-to-life/)). Camera shake is cheap impact but a fast route to nausea ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fps-game-design-fundamentals-ue5)). Hit markers are "overused and often misused" compared with target reactions ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fps/hit-markers-are-an-fps-crutch/)). For a true first-person view with a body, UE 5.5's experimental First Person Rendering draws the arms at a separate FOV. Arms warp with pitch when that FOV differs a lot from the world FOV ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/first-person-rendering-mesh-warping-issue/2697878)), and custom camera managers must set FirstPersonFOV on the view ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/5-5-first-person-rendering-fov-with-cameramanager-lyra-or-gasp/2224698)).

## One C++ base and a data asset per archetype make enemy variety cheap

Epic's free Parrot sample shows the structure. A C++ base class carries health and death and is shared with the player; a C++ enemy subclass and an AI controller sit on top. Hit volumes live in a Blueprint child, because an inherited trigger volume cannot be modified per mesh. **One shared anim template has thin child AnimBPs that only override clips.** A shared blackboard and shared tasks feed one small tree per enemy, and patrols use a reusable spline component ([Epic docs: Parrot](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/enemy-characters-in-parrot-for-unreal-engine?lang=en-US)). Commercial enemy kits, seen only through vendor snippets, push tuning further into data. Wardenmind uses one Profile data asset per enemy type for team, perception, range and cooldown ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/astore-wardenmind/2839752)). Animus Ex Machina uses one config asset per archetype on the controller ([docs](https://arconiclabs.github.io/AnimusExMachina/reference/configuration)). The Fab "Soulslike Enemy Combat System" splits StateTree strategy from action execution and assigns roles such as "Aggressor" and "Flanker" in config ([Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/7087cec0-6975-4de5-82e0-0de0b9b3e9a7)). ACF lists an "AI ticketing system" without explaining it ([Fab ACF V4](https://www.fab.com/listings/ca5f015f-4976-4928-84a1-8826954a2a57)). GAS shows up in these examples for ability selection, but nothing requires it: tag-driven C++ components plus data assets give the same variety without reopening the "No GAS" decision.

Our branch already follows this shape. It has a C++ enemy base and a StateTree whose Engage state means "reposition, then attack": Donk peeks while other roles fire bursts. There are five MetaHuman bodies. The cheap next layer is a `UPrimaryDataAsset` per archetype holding the MetaHuman preset, weapon BP, perception, ranges, role tag, aggression, cooldowns and hit-reaction set. StateTree tasks would be parameterised by Gameplay Tags instead of a tree per enemy. A shared set of damage states (leg stun, stagger, arm drop) would give every archetype Naughty Dog-style variety for free, and an attack-token budget would keep a squad from firing all at once. This is how we get "many characters with different mechanics, inspired by other games". Take a mechanic from the genre, such as a shield-bearing pusher, a flanker that smokes cover, or a heavy that suppresses. Implement it as a role plus data. Then dress it in an original KOCH silhouette. No source measured the cost of a new enemy type, and a forum thread asking how to avoid "class explosion" got no replies ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/best-approach-for-structuring-enemy-blueprints-behavior-trees-and-aicontrollers-to-avoid-class-explosion/2626619)). This structure is a recurring pattern in Epic's sample and vendor kits, not a benchmarked one.

## Copy the mechanic, never the expression or the files

Of the legal sources, only the Copyright Office games page and the Fab EULA were read in full; the court opinions and the MetaHuman, Mixamo, Meshy and Tripo terms below come from search summaries, so check the primary text before relying on exact wording. Patents and trademarks were not researched, so everything here is about copyright. US copyright law draws the line clearly at the top and blurrily at the edges. The Copyright Office states that "the idea for a game is not protected by copyright", nor are its name or "the methods for playing it". Rules text and graphic art can be registered ([Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)). *Tetris v. Xio* (D.N.J. 2012, a district court) shows the edge. The court **rejected the defence that only rules and functions were copied**, held that how a game expresses its rules is protectable, and granted summary judgment on trade dress as well ([Justia](https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-jersey/njdce/3:2009cv06115/235418/61)). *Spry Fox v. 6waves*, a clone dispute over *Triple Town*, settled without a ruling ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spry_Fox,_LLC_v._Lolapps,_Inc.)). For characters, *DC Comics v. Towle* protects a character that is sufficiently delineated, with consistent, recognisable traits; even the Batmobile qualified ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/13-55484/13-55484-2015-09-23.html)). Commentary on the Booker T. v. Activision dispute argues that stock archetypes are less protectable ([Above the Law](https://abovethelaw.com/?p=1121627)). In practice, rebuilding cover shooting, bullet time, a grappling hook or a peek-and-fire enemy is generally lawful under copyright. A recognisable copy of a named character's look, costume or catchphrase is not, even remodelled. Neither is a game's specific level layout, as DMCA Royale shows below. Our own inference is to treat a game's UI layout and distinctive numeric tuning the same way.

Ripped files are the clearest red line. EA removed the OpenSC2K remake over its ripped sprites ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/ea-takes-down-open-source-simcity-2000-remake-for-using-copyrighted-assets/)). Nintendo killed *Mario Royale* and then its reskin *DMCA Royale*, which still kept the original level layout ([Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2019/06/28/nintendo-does-nintendo-mario-royale-fan-game-becomes-dmca-royale-is-now-dead/)). Nintendo also removed hundreds of Game Jolt fan games in 2016 ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2016/09/07/nintendo-wields-dmca-axe-on-fan-games)). Valve's takedown of a TF2 Source 2 port cited ported assets ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/01/team-fortress-source-2-fan-project-gets-a-dmca-from-valve/comment_id=255938)). Free and non-profit status protected none of them. Mods "inspired by" other games, rather than ripped from them, tend to survive ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/halo/microsoft-dmca-takedown-project-misriah-halo-cs2-mod/)). So models, animations, sounds and voice lines extracted from commercial games are off the table, whatever their quality.

Licensed sources each carry their own limits. **Fab Standard** allows commercial use in any engine and needs no credit, but forbids standalone resale or redistribution ([Fab EULA](https://www.fab.com/eula)). "UE-Only Content", such as City Sample Crowds, is restricted to Unreal-based products ([Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/ada3c1dd-c4c3-4e63-b4c6-bfd010b96ad7)), which does not constrain a UE game. A forum claim that Fab's Personal tier is capped at about $100k revenue is unconfirmed ([forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/fab-license-terms-standard-license/2094243/4)). **MetaHumans** fall under the standard UE licence, free under $1M revenue ([metahuman.com](https://www.metahuman.com/en-US/license)). Since 5.6 they can even be used outside Unreal, but the 2025 terms **barred using them to train AI models** while allowing AI-assisted workflows ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/06/you-can-now-sell-metahumans-or-use-them-in-unity-or-godot/)). Epic later added an opt-in for training, and that date is unconfirmed ([Epic post](https://unrealengine.com/ue-devs-can-now-choose-to-train-ai-models-on-their-projects)). Driving MetaHumans through Claude and MCP looks like an AI-assisted workflow, not training, but nobody here has read the current EULA text. **Purely prompt-generated output is not copyrightable** in the US, while human arrangement and edits of it are ([Copyright Office](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html); [DLA Piper on Thaler](https://dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/03/copyrightability-of-genai-outputs-in-the-us-key-developments)). Meshy's free tier is CC BY 4.0 and requires attribution; paid output belongs to the user ([Meshy](https://help.meshy.ai/en/articles/9992001-can-i-use-my-generated-assets-for-commercial-projects)). Tripo's own pages contradict each other on whether free-tier output is usable at all ([Tripo help](https://www.tripo3d.ai/help/privacy-policy/how-to-use-tripo-models-commercially); [Tripo blog](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/commercial-use-ai-3d-models)). DeepMotion's free plan is reportedly non-commercial, per an unverified SEO page ([itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/compare/deepmotion-animate-3d-vs-rokoko-create/)). The biggest open gap for us is the formal licence text for **Lyra and GASP**. Neither was read, and the Epic Content License page returned 403. Epic's own invitation to retarget GASP makes "Reference Only" unlikely, but the Fab licence tab is the authority.

## Conclusion

The clips hide where the work really goes. Assets and code are now the cheap part: agents write logic quickly, and Epic gives away the mocap. What stays expensive is integration and tuning: retarget QA on new MetaHuman meshes, warp windows, hit capsules and reaction sets, and per-machine frame budgets. No source quantifies any of it, and that is where a small team's "AAA" either appears or doesn't. For us, the leverage point is a shared damage-state and hit-reaction layer. One system makes every gun feel better, gives every archetype new tactical behaviour, and costs nothing in licensing.

The evidence base is thinner than the hype suggests. The performance numbers come from one community profile, the framework claims from vendor pages, the viral claims from self-reports, and the key licence texts went unread. The most valuable next research is therefore our own: measure on machines A and B, and read the licence tabs. More web searching would add little.
